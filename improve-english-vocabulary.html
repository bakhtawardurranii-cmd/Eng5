How to Improve English Vocabulary (Without Just Memorizing Word Lists)

6 min read · By Bakhtawar Durrani, CELTA & TESOL-certified Founder · Last updated Aug 2026

Vocabulary size matters less than vocabulary retrieval speed — the real problem for most learners isn't that they don't know enough words, it's that the words they do know don't come to mind fast enough in conversation. Here's how to actually fix that, not just add more words to a list you'll forget.

Why Do Vocabulary Word Lists Rarely Work?

Flashcard apps and vocabulary lists build recognition — you can spot the word and remember its meaning when you see it. But speaking requires production: pulling the word out of memory fast enough to use it mid-sentence, without breaking your flow. These are genuinely different skills, and recognition practice alone won't build the second one.

Should You Learn Words in Isolation?

Instead of memorizing "meticulous = very careful," learn it attached to a specific, memorable sentence about your own life: "My accountant is meticulous about receipts." The personal context does more for recall than the dictionary definition ever will.

What Is the 3-Word-a-Day Rule?

Trying to learn 20 new words a week sounds efficient but rarely sticks — most get forgotten within days because there's no repetition built in. Three words a day, actually used in a spoken sentence and revisited the next day, beats twenty words skimmed once.

DayActivity
Day 1Learn 3 words, use each in one spoken sentence
Day 2Reuse yesterday's 3 words in new sentences, then add 3 more
Day 3Reuse all 6 so far, then add 3 more
Day 7Quick review of the week's 21 words in conversation

Should You Add New Words or Replace Old Ones?

A faster way to sound more advanced: identify the 10–15 words you overuse (very, good, nice, big, said) and learn one or two specific upgrades for each, rather than learning unrelated new vocabulary.

The food was very good.
→ The food was outstanding.

He is a big problem for the team.
→ He is a significant liability for the team.

Does Vocabulary Improve Just From Reading?

Reading builds recognition; speaking builds retrieval. If your goal is to sound more fluent in real conversations or in the IELTS Speaking test, the words need to be practiced out loud, ideally with someone who can tell you when a word choice doesn't quite fit — a native-sounding word used slightly wrong sounds more foreign than a plainer, correct one.

Quick answers

FAQ

Why can't I remember words I've studied when I'm actually speaking?

Studying with flashcards builds recognition, not retrieval. Speaking requires quickly producing a word from memory, which needs active practice, not just review.

How many new words should I learn per day?

Three words learned deeply and reused over several days will stick better than twenty words skimmed once and forgotten.
