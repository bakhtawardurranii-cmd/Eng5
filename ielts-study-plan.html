How to Build an IELTS Study Plan That Actually Fits Your Level

7 min read · By Bakhtawar Durrani, CELTA & TESOL-certified Founder · Last updated Aug 2026

A good IELTS study plan is built backward from your target band and your weakest module, not forward from a generic 8-week template — the same plan rarely works for two students with different starting points. Here's how to actually build one.

Step 1: Get an honest baseline

You can't build an efficient plan without knowing your real starting band across all four modules. Guessing, or relying on how confident you feel, usually misdirects your effort toward skills you're already fine at. Start with a proper diagnostic — our free mock test is built for exactly this.

Step 2: Identify the real gap, not the assumed one

Many students assume Speaking is their weak point because it feels hardest in the moment, when their actual band data shows Writing is costing them more marks. Work from your scored results, not your gut feeling.

Step 3: Build a weekly structure around your weakest 2 modules

PhaseFocus
Weeks 1–2Focus on weakest module + light daily practice on the rest
Weeks 3–5Second-weakest module added in, full practice tests begin
Week 6Full timed mock test under real conditions
Weeks 7–8Targeted correction based on mock test results, final polish

This is a typical 8-week structure for someone starting from a Band 5.5–6 aiming for Band 7 — your actual plan should adjust based on your starting point and timeline.

Step 4: Don't neglect your strongest module entirely

It's tempting to spend all your time on your weakest module, but even strong modules drift without any practice — allocate at least one light session a week to your strongest skill just to maintain it.

Step 5: Re-test before you assume you're ready

Studying for six weeks doesn't automatically mean your band has moved — the only way to know is a second mock test under the same real conditions as the first one, ideally 1–2 weeks before your actual exam date.

Is a Study Plan Enough Without Feedback?

Self-study can follow a schedule, but it can't tell you whether you're actually closing the gap — that requires being scored against real band descriptors at each checkpoint. Quick answers

FAQ

How long should an IELTS study plan be?

It depends on your starting band and target, but a common structure for moving from Band 5.5–6 to Band 7 runs about 6–8 weeks of focused preparation.

Should my study plan focus only on my weakest module?

Mostly, but not entirely — allocate at least light weekly practice to your strongest module too, since skills drift without any maintenance.
