IELTS Writing Task 2: A Time Management Strategy That Actually Works

8 min read · By Bakhtawar Durrani, CELTA & TESOL-certified Founder · Last updated Aug 2026

The most common reason students run out of time on IELTS Writing Task 2 isn't slow writing — it's spending too long deciding what to say before writing anything. Here's a timing structure that consistently works for our students.

How Should You Split Your 40 Minutes?

Task 2 is worth roughly twice as many marks as Task 1, so it deserves roughly twice the time. Within your 40 minutes for Task 2, aim for this structure:

StageTimeWhat to do
Plan5 minDecide your position and 2–3 supporting ideas before writing a single sentence.
Introduction3–4 minParaphrase the question and state your position clearly.
Body Paragraph 110 minOne main idea, explained and supported with an example.
Body Paragraph 210 minA second, distinct idea — not a repeat of the first with different words.
Conclusion3–4 minSummarize your position — don't introduce a new idea here.
Review3–5 minCheck grammar, word count, and that you actually answered the question asked.

Why Does the Planning Stage Matter Most?

Students who skip planning often write themselves into a corner halfway through paragraph two, realizing their two ideas actually overlap — then either repeat themselves or scramble for a new idea with limited time left. Five minutes of planning almost always saves more than five minutes later.

Should You Write Task 2 Before Task 1?

The exam presents Task 1 first, but nothing requires you to answer it first. Since Task 2 carries more weight, many examiners and prep specialists — including our instructors — recommend starting with Task 2 while your energy and focus are highest, then moving to the shorter Task 1.

How Do You Practice Under Real Time Pressure?

Reading about timing strategy only goes so far — the actual skill is built by writing full responses under a visible clock, repeatedly, until the structure becomes automatic. Quick answers

FAQ

Should I write Task 1 or Task 2 first?

Many prep specialists recommend Task 2 first since it's worth more marks and benefits from your highest energy and focus.

How much time should I spend planning Task 2?

About 5 minutes — it feels like a lot when the clock is running, but it prevents running out of ideas or repeating yourself halfway through.
