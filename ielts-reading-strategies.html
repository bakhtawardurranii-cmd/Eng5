IELTS Reading Strategies: How to Stop Running Out of Time

6 min read · By Bakhtawar Durrani, CELTA & TESOL-certified Founder · Last updated Aug 2026

Most students who struggle with IELTS Reading aren't struggling with English comprehension — they're running out of time because they're reading every passage word-for-word before answering any questions. The fix is a different reading strategy for different question types, not just reading faster.

Should You Skim Before Reading in Detail?

Spend 1–2 minutes skimming each passage before answering anything — read the first and last sentence of each paragraph to understand the overall structure and topic flow. This makes locating specific answers much faster than starting cold.

Does Reading Strategy Change by Question Type?

Question typeStrategy
True/False/Not GivenScan for the exact claim, then check wording carefully — these test precision, not general understanding
Matching headingsFocus on each paragraph's main idea, not every detail — usually found in the first or last sentence
Multiple choiceRead the question first, then locate that specific section rather than rereading the whole passage
Fill-in-the-blank / summary completionPredict the type of word needed (noun, verb, number) before scanning for it

"Not Given" is not the same as "False"

This single distinction accounts for a large share of avoidable Reading mistakes. False means the passage directly contradicts the statement. Not Given means the passage simply doesn't mention it at all — don't use outside knowledge or assumptions to decide.

Don't get stuck on one question

Spending five minutes on a single difficult question is the most common way students run out of time for an entire passage. Mark it, move on, and come back if time allows — a guessed answer costs nothing, but a skipped final section costs several marks.

How Should You Manage Your 60 Minutes?

SectionTarget time
Passage 1~17 minutes
Passage 2~20 minutes
Passage 3~23 minutes (usually the hardest)

How Do You Build Real Reading Speed?

Strategy helps, but it can't fully replace reading speed and vocabulary range built over time. Reading real English content regularly — news, articles, even our own blog — builds the underlying speed that makes these strategies actually work under time pressure.

Quick answers

FAQ

What's the difference between \'False\' and \'Not Given\' in IELTS Reading?

False means the passage directly contradicts the statement. Not Given means the passage doesn't mention the claim at all — don't use outside knowledge to decide.

How much time should I spend on each Reading passage?

Roughly 17 minutes on Passage 1, 20 on Passage 2, and 23 on Passage 3, since the third passage is usually the most difficult.
