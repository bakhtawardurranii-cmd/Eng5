The 4 scoring criteria

How Is IELTS Speaking Actually Marked?

Fluency & Coherence

Speaking at a natural pace, linking ideas logically, and recovering smoothly from hesitation without over-relying on fillers.

Lexical Resource

Using a varied, accurate range of vocabulary — including topic-specific words and natural paraphrasing — rather than repeating the same simple terms.

Grammatical Range & Accuracy

Mixing simple and complex sentence structures correctly, with control over tenses and clause structure under time pressure.

Pronunciation

Clear, intelligible speech with natural stress, intonation, and rhythm — not a native-like accent, but consistent clarity.

Structure

What Happens in Each Part of the Speaking Test?

01

Part 1 — Introduction & Familiar Topics

4–5 minutes of short questions about yourself, your home, work or studies, and familiar everyday topics. Examiners are checking for natural, conversational fluency here.

02

Part 2 — The Long Turn

You get a cue card with a topic, 1 minute to prepare notes, then speak for up to 2 minutes without interruption. This section rewards clear structure and the ability to sustain speech.

03

Part 3 — Two-Way Discussion

4–5 minutes of more abstract questions related to the Part 2 topic, designed to test your ability to analyze, compare, and justify opinions in more complex language.

Common mistakes

What Actually Costs Students Marks in Speaking?

Most students preparing for IELTS Speaking in Karachi lose marks in predictable places: over-rehearsed answers that don't match the actual question, one-word answers in Part 1 that give the examiner nothing to score, running out of things to say partway through the Part 2 long turn, and avoiding complex grammar out of fear of making mistakes — which actually caps your Grammatical Range score regardless of accuracy. Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How is IELTS Speaking scored?

Examiners score Speaking on four equally weighted criteria: Fluency and Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy, and Pronunciation. Your overall Speaking band is the average of these four, so weakness in one area caps your whole score.

How long is the IELTS Speaking test?

11 to 14 minutes, split into three parts: a short introduction and general questions (Part 1), a 2-minute individual long turn on a cue card topic (Part 2), and a two-way discussion on related, more abstract themes (Part 3).

Do I need a large vocabulary to get a high Speaking band?

Less than you'd think. Band 7+ depends more on using a range of vocabulary naturally and accurately, including some less common words and idiomatic language, than on memorizing long word lists. Our vocabulary checker can show you where your active vocabulary currently sits.

Is it better to speak fast in the Speaking test?

No. Fluency means speaking at a natural pace with minimal hesitation, not speed. Rushing usually increases pronunciation and grammar errors, which lowers your score on those two criteria.

Can I prepare answers in advance for IELTS Speaking?

You can prepare structures and vocabulary, but memorizing full scripted answers is risky — examiners are trained to recognize rehearsed responses, and it can hurt your Fluency and Coherence score if the memorized version doesn't fit the actual question asked.
