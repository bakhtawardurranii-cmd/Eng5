Which one you need

How Do I Know Which Version to Take?

This depends entirely on your purpose for taking the test, not personal preference. Universities and most professional bodies require Academic. Immigration authorities in the UK, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand generally require General Training for most visa categories. Always confirm the exact requirement with the specific university, employer, or immigration authority before booking — the wrong version can't be swapped after your test date.

Preparation

Does Preparation Differ Between the Two?

Listening and Speaking preparation is identical for both versions, so that work always carries over. Reading and Writing preparation needs to be specific to your version — our IELTS Preparation program covers both, and we confirm which version you need at the start so every session is built around the right materials.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the actual difference between IELTS Academic and General Training?

Listening and Speaking are identical in both versions. Reading and Writing differ: Academic uses longer, more complex texts and asks you to describe a chart or graph in Writing Task 1, while General Training uses shorter, everyday texts and asks for a letter in Task 1.

Which version do I need — Academic or General Training?

Academic is required for university admission and most professional registration. General Training is used for immigration to countries like the UK, Canada, and Australia, and for secondary education or work experience purposes. Always confirm the required version with the specific institution or immigration authority first.

Is General Training easier than Academic?

General Training Reading and Writing are generally considered less demanding, which is reflected in a stricter conversion table — meaning you typically need more correct answers in General Training Reading to achieve the same band as Academic Reading.

Can I switch from one version to the other later?

Yes, since Listening and Speaking preparation carries over fully. Only your Reading and Writing practice needs to shift focus if you change which version you're taking.

Do both versions use the same band scale?

Yes. Both versions are scored on the same 1–9 band scale, and both use the same Speaking and Listening tests — only the Reading and Writing content and scoring tables differ.
