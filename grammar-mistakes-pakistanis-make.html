Grammar Mistakes Pakistanis Commonly Make in English (and Why They Happen)

7 min read · By Bakhtawar Durrani, CELTA & TESOL-certified Founder · Last updated Aug 2026

Most recurring English grammar mistakes among Pakistani speakers trace back to a handful of structural differences between Urdu and English — not a lack of studying. Once you see the pattern behind a mistake, it's much easier to catch yourself making it.

Which Preposition Mistakes Come From Urdu Phrasing?

Urdu prepositions don't map one-to-one onto English ones, which is why errors like these are so common and so persistent even among otherwise strong speakers.

I am married with a doctor.
→ I am married to a doctor.

Listen my advice.
→ Listen to my advice.

I am good in English.
→ I am good at English.

What Are Double Comparative and Missing Article Mistakes?

Two structural habits that persist even at intermediate levels — stacking comparative words, and dropping "the" where English requires it.

This is more better than the old plan.
→ This is better than the old plan.

I am knowing the answer.
→ I know the answer.

What Are Double Subject Mistakes?

Certain Urdu sentence patterns carry over as unnecessary repetition in English.

My father, he works in Dubai.
→ My father works in Dubai.

Return back the book.
→ Return the book.

What's the Countable vs. Uncountable Noun Mistake?

Urdu doesn't distinguish countable and uncountable nouns the way English does, which leads to mistakes around plurals and quantity words.

I have many furnitures.
→ I have a lot of furniture.

Give me an advice.
→ Give me some advice.

Why Do Questions Need 'Do,' 'Does,' or 'Did'?

English requires "do/does/did" in most questions — a step Urdu sentence structure doesn't need, which is why this mistake persists even at intermediate levels.

You want tea?
→ Do you want tea?

Why you are late?
→ Why are you late?

What's the Fastest Way to Fix These Mistakes?

None of these mistakes are about intelligence or effort — they're structural habits formed early, and they need to be corrected the same way they were formed: through repeated, corrected speaking practice, not just reading a list like this one. A tutor who hears you use one of these patterns in real conversation and corrects it on the spot will fix it faster than any amount of solo grammar review.

Quick answers

FAQ

Why do these specific grammar mistakes happen so consistently?

Most come directly from Urdu sentence structures that don't map one-to-one onto English grammar rules, especially around prepositions, tense, and question formation.

Can reading about these mistakes actually fix them?

It helps you recognize the pattern, but fixing the habit requires speaking practice with real-time correction, since these are ingrained speech habits, not knowledge gaps.
