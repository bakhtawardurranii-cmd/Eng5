IELTS Speaking Mistakes That Quietly Cost You Band Score

6 min read · By Bakhtawar Durrani, CELTA & TESOL-certified Founder · Last updated Aug 2026

The most common IELTS Speaking mistake isn't grammar — it's giving answers that are too short, which gives the examiner nothing to score you on. A grammatically perfect one-sentence answer scores lower than a longer, slightly imperfect one that actually demonstrates range.

Are Your IELTS Speaking Answers Too Short?

Part 1 and Part 3 both reward extended answers with reasoning, not just a direct fact.

Do you like your job? Yes, I like it.
→ Yes, I really enjoy it, mainly because it involves a lot of variety — no two days look the same, which keeps things interesting.

Should You Memorize IELTS Speaking Answers?

Examiners are trained to notice when a candidate delivers a pre-memorized answer that doesn't quite match what was asked — it disrupts natural intonation and often ignores part of the question. Prepare ideas and vocabulary, not full scripted answers.

What If You Run Out of Things to Say in Part 2?

The cue card gives you bullet points specifically so you don't run out of material — use every one of them, and add specific details (names, numbers, feelings) rather than staying general.

StyleExample
Too generalIt was a good day. I enjoyed it a lot.
Specific and scoreableIt was the day my cousin got married in Hyderabad — the food, the music, everyone dancing until 2am.

Do Filler Words Hurt Your IELTS Speaking Score?

Occasional natural hesitation is fine and even expected — but repeated "um," "like," and long silent pauses hurt your Fluency and Coherence score. Learn a few natural stalling phrases instead.

Um... um... I think... um...
→ That's an interesting question — let me think for a second...

Does Self-Correcting Hurt Your Speaking Score?

Self-correcting once or twice actually helps your score by showing control of the language — but constant restarting mid-sentence disrupts fluency and signals nervousness rather than accuracy.

What If You Don't Know Much About the Topic?

If you don't know much about a topic, examiners still expect you to attempt a full answer rather than giving up early — improvising confidently scores better than a short, safe non-answer.

How Do You Actually Fix These Speaking Habits?

These are performance habits, not knowledge gaps — they only show up and get fixed through practicing the actual Speaking format under timed, examiner-style conditions. That's exactly how the Speaking module works in our free IELTS mock test and our IELTS Preparation program.

Quick answers

FAQ

Is it bad to use memorized answers in IELTS Speaking?

Yes — examiners are trained to notice memorized answers, especially when they don't quite fit the question asked. Prepare ideas and vocabulary instead of scripted answers.

What should I do if I don't know much about a Part 2 topic?

Attempt a full answer anyway, improvising with related ideas — a confident, complete answer scores better than a short, safe one that avoids the topic.
